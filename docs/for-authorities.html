For public authorities · Ready to review

Stockpiles tell us what exists and where. FoodAllo is the rule for who gets what, and when.

FoodAllo is a free, public-interest allocation layer for food and life-essential non-food when markets fail. It sits next to national and local preparedness — stockpiles, crisis law, and aid — and does not replace them. This is not a commercial offer.

Who this page is for

Ministries, municipalities, preparedness units, and coordination desks that must keep fair distribution workable when money alone no longer clears the market — including people without smartphones.

The gap FoodAllo addresses

  • Reserves and logistics say what is available and where it sits — not who is entitled to how much under shortage.
  • First-come issue empties warehouses and leaves infants, children, and older people behind.
  • Digital-only tools exclude people who need desk, proxy, or paper access.
  • Without published rules, crisis allocation becomes opaque and hard to defend.

What FoodAllo adds as a layer

  • Need-based shares: basic amounts, with infants, children and older people first when stock is tight.
  • Household caps so hoarding cannot empty the warehouse.
  • Multi-channel access: app, web, municipal/retail desk, neighbour/proxy, paper emergency codes — same max share.
  • Transparent rules you can walk through before a crisis, including why other paths fail.
  • A staged path: paper start → limited digital → shared picture of stock and shortages — not a big-bang IT programme.

What FoodAllo is not

  • Not a claim to end all hunger or produce food
  • Not a substitute for crisis law, national stockpiles, or humanitarian pipelines
  • Not standing welfare or consumer surveillance profiling
  • Not a paid product, paid advertising, or a pitch that locks you to one vendor
  • Not an official government system and not an impersonation of one

How it fits preparedness work

Treat FoodAllo as the missing who-gets-what rule beside existing instruments: stockpiles for availability, law for authority, and fair issue under shortage. The public pages are shared rule walkthroughs for technical and policy review, not an official live service — free and no account required.
